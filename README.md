# LearnLoop Marketing Analytics Platform

**End-to-End Marketing Analytics | SQL · Tableau · Snowflake · dbt · Python · Airflow · AWS S3**

---

## Project Overview

**LearnLoop** is a simulated subscription-based EdTech SaaS company that sells online courses and corporate training through subscription plans.

The company acquires customers through Google Ads and Meta Ads, but its **advertising, website, CRM, subscription, payment, and course data are distributed across separate systems**.

This makes it difficult to answer a fundamental business question:

> **Which marketing investments are actually generating customers and business value?**

I built an end-to-end analytics platform that connects the customer journey from:

**Ad Spend → Website Sessions → Leads → MQLs → SQLs → Customers → Subscriptions → Revenue**

Using **Python, AWS S3, Snowflake, dbt, SQL, Airflow, and Tableau**, the project centralizes these sources and transforms them into business-ready KPIs and dashboards for marketing decision-making.

---

# Key Findings

The analysis revealed several important opportunities for LearnLoop.

| Finding | Result |
|---|---:|
| Advertising Spend | **$1.11M** |
| Attributed Revenue | **$373.5K** |
| Overall ROAS | **0.34x** |
| Website Sessions | **740,283** |
| Leads | **93,276** |
| Customers | **9,592** |
| Customer Acquisition Cost | **$116.08** |
| Subscriptions | **9,592** |
| Course Enrollments | **16,628** |

### 1. Marketing spend is not currently producing enough attributed revenue

LearnLoop spent approximately **$1.11M on advertising** while generating approximately **$373.5K in attributed revenue**.

This produces an overall:

**ROAS ≈ 0.34x**

In other words, for every **$1.00 invested in advertising, approximately $0.34 of attributed revenue was generated** within the revenue measurement used by the analysis.

This is the clearest indication that the current marketing mix needs optimization.

---

### 2. Customer acquisition is expensive relative to the observed revenue

The company acquired **9,592 customers** from approximately **$1.11M in advertising spend**, resulting in:

**CAC = $116.08**

CAC should not be evaluated independently. It needs to be compared with the revenue and lifetime value generated by acquired customers.

The combination of **high CAC and 0.34x overall ROAS** indicates that LearnLoop should focus less on maximizing acquisition volume and more on acquiring customers efficiently.

---

### 3. The top of the funnel is large, but substantial drop-off occurs before purchase

The acquisition funnel generated:

**740,283 Sessions → 93,276 Leads → 9,592 Customers**

This means approximately:

- **12.6% of sessions generated a lead**
- **10.3% of leads became customers**
- **1.3% of sessions ultimately became customers**

The company therefore has a large volume of traffic, but only a small percentage ultimately reaches the customer stage.

Improving funnel conversion could increase customer acquisition without requiring the same proportional increase in advertising spend.

---

### 4. Campaigns should not be evaluated using traffic metrics alone

The campaign analysis shows why clicks and impressions are insufficient for marketing decisions.

A campaign can generate significant traffic while still producing weak financial performance.

Campaigns should therefore be compared using downstream metrics such as:

**Spend → Leads → Customers → CAC → Revenue → ROAS → Customer Value**

This allows LearnLoop to distinguish campaigns that generate activity from campaigns that generate actual business value.

---

### 5. Customer value should influence marketing allocation

The Subscription & Customer Value analysis extends campaign measurement beyond the initial conversion.

Two campaigns that acquire the same number of customers may have very different business value if one produces customers with stronger subscription revenue or lifetime value.

Marketing investment should therefore consider both:

**Acquisition Efficiency + Customer Value**

rather than acquisition volume alone.

---

### 6. Course engagement provides an additional measure of customer quality

The dataset contains **16,628 course enrollments** generated by LearnLoop subscribers.

Course completion and enrollment behavior provide another way to understand the quality of acquired customers.

Customers who actively use the product may represent more valuable acquisition than customers who subscribe but show little engagement.

# Recommendations

Based on the analysis, I would recommend the following actions to maximize the return on LearnLoop's marketing investment.

## 1. Reallocate budget toward campaigns with stronger ROAS

LearnLoop should avoid distributing advertising budget based primarily on impressions, clicks, or traffic.

Campaigns should be ranked using:

**ROAS + CAC + Customer Value**

Budget should gradually move toward campaigns that:

- Generate higher revenue relative to spend
- Acquire customers at lower CAC
- Generate higher-value customers

Low-performing campaigns should be reduced, redesigned, or paused.

---

## 2. Set a minimum ROAS target for paid campaigns

The current overall ROAS is approximately:

**0.34x**

That means attributed revenue is substantially below advertising spend.

LearnLoop should establish campaign-level ROAS thresholds and regularly identify:

**Scale → Optimize → Reduce → Pause**

campaigns based on financial performance.

This prevents budget from continuing to flow toward campaigns that generate activity but insufficient business value.

---

## 3. Improve funnel conversion before aggressively increasing traffic

LearnLoop already generated approximately **740K website sessions**.

However, only about **1.3% of sessions ultimately became customers**.

This suggests that simply purchasing additional traffic may not be the highest-value next step.

The company should test improvements to:

- Landing pages
- Calls to action
- Lead forms
- Free-trial experiences
- Lead nurturing
- MQL qualification
- Sales follow-up

Even modest improvements in conversion could generate more customers from the traffic LearnLoop is already paying for.

---

## 4. Optimize CAC together with CLV

CAC should not be minimized blindly.

A campaign with a higher CAC may still be attractive if it acquires customers with significantly higher lifetime value.

LearnLoop should evaluate campaigns using:

**CLV / CAC**

and prioritize acquisition sources that generate the strongest long-term economics.

---

## 5. Optimize for downstream value, not just leads

Generating more leads is useful only when those leads eventually become valuable customers.

Campaign optimization should therefore move beyond:

**Clicks → Leads**

toward:

**Clicks → Leads → Customers → Subscriptions → Revenue → CLV**

This gives marketing teams a much stronger basis for allocating budget.

---

## 6. Use product engagement as an additional customer-quality signal

Marketing and product analytics should be connected.

If particular campaigns or subscription plans consistently acquire customers with stronger course enrollment and completion behavior, those acquisition sources may deserve additional investment.

This creates a broader definition of marketing success:

**Efficient Acquisition + Revenue + Retention/Value + Product Engagement**

# Business Questions & Answers

## 1. Which campaigns generate the highest ROI and ROAS?

Campaign performance varies significantly, meaning marketing budget should not be distributed equally across campaigns.

The analysis compares each campaign's:

- Ad Spend
- Revenue
- ROAS
- CAC
- Customer Acquisition

The overall portfolio generates approximately:

**$373.5K Revenue / $1.11M Ad Spend = 0.34x ROAS**

Because the aggregate ROAS is below 1.0x, LearnLoop should use the campaign-level dashboard to identify stronger performers and reallocate budget away from campaigns with weak returns.

**Business decision:** Increase investment in campaigns with stronger ROAS and customer value while optimizing or reducing inefficient campaigns.

---

## 2. What is Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)?

LearnLoop spent approximately:

**$1.11M**

to acquire:

**9,592 customers**

resulting in:

**CAC ≈ $116.08 per customer**

**Business decision:** CAC should be monitored by campaign and compared with CLV rather than evaluated only at the company level.

---

## 3. Which marketing channels generate the most valuable customers?

Channel and campaign performance should be evaluated using downstream customer outcomes rather than traffic alone.

The platform connects campaign acquisition with customer and subscription data, allowing LearnLoop to compare:

- Customers acquired
- CAC
- Revenue
- Subscription value
- Customer value

**Business decision:** Prioritize channels that produce profitable/high-value customers rather than simply the highest traffic volume.

---

## 4. Where do potential customers drop off in the acquisition funnel?

The funnel contains:

| Funnel Stage | Volume |
|---|---:|
| Website Sessions | **740,283** |
| Leads | **93,276** |
| Customers | **9,592** |

Approximately:

**12.6% of sessions become leads**

and:

**10.3% of leads become customers**

Overall:

**~1.3% of sessions become customers**

The detailed Tableau funnel additionally tracks the intermediate **MQL and SQL stages**.

**Business decision:** Improving conversion between funnel stages could increase customers without requiring an equivalent increase in advertising spend.

---

## 5. What is the Lead-to-Customer Conversion Rate?

Using:

**9,592 Customers / 93,276 Leads**

the overall Lead-to-Customer Conversion Rate is approximately:

**10.3%**

This means roughly **1 out of every 10 generated leads becomes a customer**.

**Business decision:** Improve lead qualification, nurturing, and conversion processes while also analyzing which campaigns produce leads with the highest probability of becoming customers.

---

## 6. What is Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)?

CLV is calculated in the customer-value analytics layer and visualized in the **Subscription & Customer Value** dashboard.

Rather than using CLV independently, the most important business application is comparing it against CAC:

**Customer Value vs. Acquisition Cost**

This helps identify whether the customers generated by a campaign justify the amount spent to acquire them.

**Business decision:** Allocate more budget toward campaigns that produce stronger customer value relative to CAC.

---

## 7. Which courses generate the most enrollments and engagement?

LearnLoop generated:

**16,628 course enrollments**

The Course Performance dashboard compares:

- Enrollments by Course
- Enrollments by Category
- Completion Rate by Course
- Enrollments Over Time

**Business decision:** Use enrollment and completion behavior to identify which educational products attract and engage customers most effectively.

---

## 8. How are MRR and ARR changing over time?

The Subscription & Customer Value dashboard tracks:

- MRR
- ARR
- MRR Over Time
- Active Subscriptions

These metrics allow LearnLoop to monitor the recurring-revenue health of the subscription business instead of relying only on one-time payment revenue.

**Business decision:** Track whether customer acquisition is translating into sustainable recurring-revenue growth.

---

## 9. Which subscription plans generate the most recurring revenue?

The dashboard compares:

- Subscriptions by Plan
- MRR by Plan
- Active Subscriptions
- Customer Value

Because plans have different prices, the plan with the largest number of subscribers is not necessarily the plan generating the most recurring revenue.

**Business decision:** Evaluate subscription plans using both customer volume and recurring-revenue contribution.

---

## 10. How should marketing performance ultimately be measured?

The project demonstrates that marketing performance should not stop at:

**Impressions → Clicks**

Instead, LearnLoop should evaluate the full customer lifecycle:

**Ad Spend → Sessions → Leads → MQLs → SQLs → Customers → Subscriptions → Revenue → Customer Value**

This provides a much stronger basis for deciding where the next marketing dollar should be invested.

# ETL & Analytics Pipeline

1. **Python** extracts and validates source data.
2. Raw JSON data is stored in **AWS S3**.
3. Data is loaded into **Snowflake**.
4. **dbt staging models** clean and standardize source data.
5. **Intermediate models** combine data across systems.
6. **Fact and dimension tables** organize the analytical warehouse.
7. **dbt tests** validate data quality.
8. **SQL analytics models** calculate business KPIs.
9. **Apache Airflow** orchestrates the workflow.
10. **Tableau** presents the final business analysis.
